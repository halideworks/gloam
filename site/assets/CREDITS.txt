Image credits and provenance for the Gloam site
===============================================

compare_windows_srgb.webp
compare_gloam_gamma2p2.webp
---------------------------

The before/after pair in the "01 / The problem" section of index.html.

Source
  Georges de La Tour (French, 1593-1652)
  "The Penitent Magdalen", ca. 1640
  Oil on canvas, 133.4 x 102.2 cm
  The Metropolitan Museum of Art, object 436839
  Gift of Mr. and Mrs. Charles Wrightsman, 1978
  https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/436839
  Scan: 2947 x 4000, sRGB.

License
  The painting is long out of copyright, and the Met releases its
  reproduction under Open Access / CC0:
  https://www.metmuseum.org/policy/open-access
  No attribution is legally required; the page credits it anyway.

Why this image
  The shadow lift Gloam corrects is largest, in code-value terms, for
  content sitting just above black -- about +7 to +8 codes across the
  1-5% signal range, tapering to nothing by 40%. A single-candle
  chiaroscuro scene puts a lot of tonal mass exactly there: roughly half
  the frame sits below 1% display light, while the candle flames clip
  white. So the correction is visible as the background recovering its
  depth, with the flames provably unmoved -- which is the actual claim
  the section makes.

  The crop is a 16:10 band through the candle, hands and skull, inset
  from the gold frame edge and the black border of the museum scan.

How the pair was made
  scripts/render-comparison-images.py, which ports the transfer-function
  math 1:1 from src/HDRGammaController.Core/TransferFunctions.cs and
  LutGenerator.cs. Both frames come from the same source pixels; the only
  difference is the decode curve:

    compare_windows_srgb.webp    what an HDR display emits when Windows
                                 decodes the SDR frame with the piecewise
                                 sRGB curve
    compare_gloam_gamma2p2.webp  what it emits with Gloam's gamma 2.2
                                 GPU LUT applied

  The frame is encoded to PQ at a 200-nit SDR white level and quantized
  to the 10-bit wire before either branch, so the simulation includes the
  real signal path rather than just the curve algebra. The emitted-light
  ratios between the two reproduce the figures quoted on the page:
  2.87x at 5% signal, 1.59x at 10%, 1.14x at 20%, 1.00x at 40% and above.
  Deeper than the page's lowest stat card the ratio keeps climbing --
  8.7x at 2% signal, 60x at code value 1 -- though in absolute nits those
  are small differences.

  Both are re-encoded for an ordinary SDR reader on the assumption that
  their display follows a ~gamma 2.2 EOTF -- the same premise the page
  and the app rest on. Under that assumption the Gloam branch is the
  untouched source and the Windows branch is the lifted one, which is the
  honest direction: Windows *adds* light to shadows relative to
  mastering intent.

  Re-render with:
    python scripts/render-comparison-images.py
  after placing magdalen.jpg (the Met's original-size scan) beside it.

  WebP quality 92, 4:4:4, no chroma subsampling. Verified: the 8px-
  integrated difference between the two frames survives compression to
  within 0.19 code values against a mean difference of 4.8, so the effect
  the page demonstrates is not a compression artifact.
